Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

At what age should students start career counselling?

Many students benefit from early exploration in Grades 8–10, when subject choices begin to take shape. Formal career and university planning is especially valuable in Grades 11–12. There is no single “right” age — the best time is when the student (or family) feels ready to explore with support.

Does counselling tell my child which career to choose?

No. Our role is to help the student understand themselves, explore possibilities and make informed decisions. We do not prescribe a single career. The student remains the decision-maker; we provide structure, information and reflection.

Do parents attend the sessions?

Parents are valued partners. Sessions can include parent-only time, student-only time, or joint conversations, depending on what is most helpful. We always protect the student’s space to speak openly while keeping parents informed and involved.

How many sessions does a student need?

It varies. Some students gain clarity in a few focused sessions; others benefit from a longer process with follow-ups. After an initial conversation we can suggest a realistic pathway. There is no fixed package that every student must buy.

Do you provide psychometric assessments?

Where appropriate, structured assessments or reflection tools may be used to explore interests, strengths and preferences. Results are always discussed within the wider counselling conversation and are never treated as a definitive career answer.

Can you help students who want to study abroad?

Yes. We provide structured guidance on exploring international options, understanding entry requirements and preparing for applications. We do not guarantee admissions or visas; we help students and families make informed choices and prepare thoroughly.

Can students who are already in university use the service?

Yes. University students who are reconsidering their course, exploring career alignment or planning next steps are welcome. Career transition guidance is designed for this stage.

Is counselling confidential?

Yes. Student conversations are treated with confidentiality within the limits of professional and legal responsibility. We discuss with families how information will be shared so everyone feels safe and respected.
